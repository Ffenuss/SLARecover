# Phase 0 Written Problem Interview — Email Q&A

Purpose: complete the Phase 0 problem interview entirely asynchronously. A meeting is not required.

Do not request credentials, secrets or raw customer data in this questionnaire.

## Email-ready questionnaire

Thanks for being open to helping with the SLARecover validation. Short answers are completely fine.

1. What is your role in AWS operations, managed services, FinOps, cloud commercial management or billing?

2. Roughly what AWS scope do you manage?
   - one environment;
   - multiple accounts/business units;
   - multiple customer environments;
   - other.

3. In the last 12 months, have you seen material AWS incidents affecting EC2, RDS, S3 or other services? If yes, roughly how often?

4. When such an incident happens, does anyone currently check whether an AWS SLA service credit may be available? If yes, who owns that process?

5. Have you or your customers actually submitted AWS SLA/service-credit claims before? If yes, what was difficult or time-consuming?

6. What evidence is normally retained after an incident?
   Examples: application/request logs, monitoring data, incident timelines, AWS Support references, billing/CUR data.

7. Does evidence ever expire or become unavailable before somebody reviews SLA eligibility?

8. Would a reproducible output showing the applicable historical SLA, exact evidence used, calculation, claim deadline and Claim Pack be useful? Why or why not?

9. Do you have one historical EC2 incident that could be used for a free manual audit?
   A first screening can use redacted identifiers. Do not email credentials or secrets.

10. If you cannot provide a case, what is the main blocker?
    Examples: no incidents, no evidence, customer confidentiality, no internal owner, existing process already works, low expected value.

## Completion rule

Count the written interview as completed only if the response establishes enough facts to assess:
- role/fit;
- AWS scope;
- incident occurrence;
- current SLA-credit workflow;
- evidence retention/readiness;
- buyer/user value signal;
- real-case availability or a concrete blocker.

Do not count a generic expression of interest as a completed interview or design-partner commitment.

## Conversion to design partner

A written design-partner commitment requires willingness to provide or safely facilitate one real/representative AWS case, allow a deterministic manual audit, and review the result. A meeting is never required.
