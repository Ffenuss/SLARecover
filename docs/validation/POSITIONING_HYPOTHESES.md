# Phase 0 Positioning Hypotheses

These are interview/pilot hypotheses, not marketing claims.

## Category reality

Direct competitors already offer combinations of:
- automated SLA monitoring;
- credit calculation;
- claim kits;
- auto-filing;
- cloud + SaaS breadth;
- MSP/partner programs;
- subscription + success fee pricing.

Therefore “automated SLA credits” is not sufficient positioning.

## Candidate wedge to test

**Audit-grade SLA recovery for high-spend cloud/MSP environments where finance, security and legal teams need every credit decision to be reproducible.**

The differentiator would be the combination of:
- historical SLA versioning;
- immutable source snapshots/hashes;
- deterministic financial calculations;
- explicit evidence sufficiency states;
- immutable evidence hashes;
- historical replay;
- human approval as the default;
- separation of monitoring and submission permissions.

Do not claim this is unique until interviews and deeper competitor diligence support it.

## Interview tests

Ask target users:
1. Who must approve an SLA claim internally?
2. What would that approver need to reproduce the calculation?
3. Would a vendor-health incident plus estimated credit be sufficient? If not, what is missing?
4. Is an evidence hash/source snapshot useful or irrelevant?
5. Would they allow an external product to auto-submit support cases?
6. Would they prefer an evidence-ready Claim Pack with explicit human approval?
7. What makes an SLA claim auditable enough for finance/procurement/legal?
8. Have they ever rejected an internal savings/recovery number because the source or formula could not be reproduced?
9. For an MSP, what audit artifacts must be shown separately to each client?
10. Would these controls justify choosing a narrower product over a cheaper broad monitoring tool?

## Falsifier

If target users repeatedly prefer broad automated coverage and do not assign meaningful value to reproducibility, provenance, evidence state or permission separation, this wedge is weak and must be changed before Phase 1.
